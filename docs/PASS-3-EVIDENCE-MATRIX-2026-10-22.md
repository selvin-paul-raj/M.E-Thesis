# AVA Evaluation — Pass 3 evidence matrix (working, 22 October 2026)

## Decision at this checkpoint

The material reviewed so far does not support claiming that AVA is a novel four-class assertion-pair benchmark, a new conflict-preserving memory design, or the first use of abstention/verification for agent memory. MELD overlaps the four-way relation taxonomy and false-merge reporting, although its 104-pair diagnostic gold set deliberately uses distinct claim keys and therefore is not a strictly same-key pair test. StateFuse and TOKI overlap conflict preservation, provenance/time-aware resolution, projection or write policies, deterministic decisions, and abstention/confirmation; LatticeMind and Memanto describe keyed or same-type memory conflict handling. TANGLE and Selective QA overlap conflict-aware action, calibration, and selective abstention. Governed Shared Memory directly studies asynchronous conflict supersession for same subject-predicate writes in a live multi-agent service, but not a pair classifier or AVA-style policy comparison. These threats make broad novelty claims unsafe; they do not prove exact preemption of the narrower AVA task.

A narrower candidate question is whether a deterministic evidence-based Verify stage, placed after classification of same-key assertions, changes the safety–resolution trade-off against fixed-winner selection and preserving every disagreement unresolved, under an asynchronous event simulator. This is only a candidate for continued falsification. It is not an admitted gap or a novelty finding. The required full-text Pass 3 is not complete; do not implement the protocol or draft the thesis/paper yet.

The three working hypotheses are tracked exactly in `AEON/departments/research/research-gaps.md`:

- WH-1: “There may be a defensible empirical question about classifying same-key assertional pairs and choosing Agree, Verify or Abstain under evidence constraints, compared across deterministic, semantic, structured and generative methods.”
- WH-2: “Confidence-aware policies may expose a safety-versus-resolution trade-off, including false-merge and false-conflict rates, not captured by accuracy alone.”
- WH-3: “A deterministic semantic-conflict evaluation under asynchronous distributed conditions may add value beyond a classification benchmark.”

All three remain provisional. The matrix below records selected primary-source sections/excerpts reviewed, not source-wide, end-to-end reading. “Full text available” means a readable full-text version was obtained; it does not mean every section, appendix, or supplement was read. No source is marked fully verified at the project's level-P standard in this checkpoint.

## Quantitative-claim corrections

1. CodeCRDT’s ~5–10% is from manual review of 60 of 600 runs. The authors call the measure preliminary, report large task variation, and say comprehensive measurement is needed. Do not describe it as a validated persistent semantic-conflict rate.
2. SCF’s 65.2% is recall, not generic “detection” accuracy; 27.9% is precision. The paper reports F1 39.0 and states 72% of detections were false positives. Its 600-run simulated workflow task is not AVA’s same-key pair task.
3. The deterministic +10.8 to +21 points comes from a full candidate-extraction/Python-aggregation answer pipeline against an LLM-judgment pipeline on a serial-numbered freshness task. The paper says the comparison changes resolver, prompt, output format and temperature jointly; it does not isolate the resolver. The 262K abstract comparison is +21, while the HTML also reports +19.8 in a different matched chunk-4096 setup. The LongMemEval n=45 check does not show a deterministic win (57.8% versus 64.4%). Do not generalize to all semantic conflict resolution.
