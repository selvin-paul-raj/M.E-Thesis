# AVA Evaluation — Pass 3 evidence matrix (working, 22 October 2026)

## Decision at this checkpoint

The material reviewed so far does not support claiming that AVA is a novel four-class assertion-pair benchmark, a new conflict-preserving memory design, or the first use of abstention/verification for agent memory. MELD overlaps the four-way relation taxonomy and false-merge reporting, although its 104-pair diagnostic gold set deliberately uses distinct claim keys and therefore is not a strictly same-key pair test. Its status CRDT converges after partition heal, but its delivery-order-sensitive merge graph, live claim set, and retrieval view do not; AVA must state which semantic state it claims to reconcile. StateFuse and TOKI overlap conflict preservation, provenance/time-aware resolution, projection or write policies, deterministic decisions, and abstention/confirmation; LatticeMind and Memanto describe keyed or same-type memory conflict handling. TANGLE and Selective QA overlap conflict-aware action, calibration, and selective abstention. Governed Shared Memory directly studies asynchronous conflict supersession for same subject-predicate writes in a live multi-agent service, but not a pair classifier or AVA-style policy comparison. These threats make broad novelty claims unsafe; they do not prove exact preemption of the narrower AVA task.

A narrower candidate question is whether a deterministic evidence-based Verify stage, placed after classification of same-key assertions, changes the safety–resolution trade-off against fixed-winner selection and preserving every disagreement unresolved, under an asynchronous event simulator. This is only a candidate for continued falsification. It is not an admitted gap or a novelty finding. The five-source primary-text audit below is now recorded, but overall Pass 3, the wider closest-threat/NLI review, active disconfirmation, and level-P reconciliation remain incomplete; do not implement the protocol or draft the thesis/paper yet.

The supplied specification names WH-1, WH-2 and WH-3 but does not define them. Earlier repository prose that presented proposed wording as exact was agent-authored and is not authoritative; the corresponding wording has been removed from the active research-gap record. Do not infer or invent those definitions. Exact falsification against the three identifiers remains open until an authoritative definition is supplied. Reading scopes for the five papers below are recorded, but some remain selected, section-only, or incomplete; the matrix does not establish a source-wide primary-text pass with exact section and appendix coverage for all five. Reading the main text and available appendices does not by itself establish the project's level-P verification standard. The overall Pass 3 gate remains open: wider prior-art/NLI checks, the active disconfirmation matrix, and the level-P reconciliation remain unfinished. No source is marked fully verified at the project's level-P standard in this checkpoint.

## Quantitative-claim corrections

1. CodeCRDT’s ~5–10% is from manual review of 60 of 600 runs. The authors call the measure preliminary, report large task variation, and say comprehensive measurement is needed. Do not describe it as a validated persistent semantic-conflict rate.
2. SCF’s 65.2% is recall, not generic “detection” accuracy; 27.9% is precision. The paper reports F1 39.0 and states 72% of detections were false positives. Its 600-run simulated workflow task is not AVA’s same-key pair task.

The specification calls this a target theorem/open proof obligation, not a proven or implemented result.
- **Governed Shared Memory** (arXiv:2606.24535v1) is the closest of these two on same-key asynchronous writes: its live MemClaw service detects different RDF objects for a single-valued subject/predicate and asynchronously supersedes after commit. The synchronous near-duplicate gate rejected 206 of 400 planned writes before they could be admitted. This is meaningful prior art for same-key supersession, provenance and the admission-vs-detection distinction. - **LatticeMind** (arXiv:2608.08236v1) has a closer structured-memory/policy overlap: it stores keyed claims with evidence, time and explicit Proposed/Confirmed/Contested/Superseded states; canonical software-state keys include entity, slot, branch and environment. It separates Credibility from Coordination conflicts, uses evidence strength then recency (LLM tie-break only afterward) for credibility updates, preserves weaker claims as contested/superseded, and leaves coordination conflicts visible. That challenges broad claims to novel conflict-aware keyed memory or classify-then-resolve policy. Its Credibility/Coordination distinction is not AVA’s four relation labels; the 75-example ConflictBank result is a multi-source answer-selection task, not an isolated same-key pair-classification result. The repository-replay study evaluates extract/compare/update, and Appendix A.9 says item-state transitions are applied centrally by the orchestrator. No asynchronous writer/replica-order convergence evaluation is reported in the inspected source.

**Residual conclusion:** these papers make broad novelty claims about same-key conflict handling, evidence-weighted supersession, conflict preservation, or abstention unsafe. They do not, on the evidence inspected, demonstrate the exact combination in the locked AVA spec: four same-key relation labels, a separately evaluated Agree/Verify/Abstain policy with deterministic evidence-only Verify for convergence-bearing arms, and event-set projection under asynchronous delivery faults. That combination remains a candidate research distinction—not a novelty result—because other prior art, the wider NLI/closest-threat matrix, level-P reconciliation, independent reproduction and authoritative WH-1/2/3 definitions remain open.
